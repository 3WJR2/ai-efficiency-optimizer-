## Objection Handling Response

### "Too Expensive"

```
I understand budget is important. Let's look at the actual cost of NOT having Qodo:

**Your current state:**
- [X] developers at $[Y] average salary = $[total] in labor
- [Z]% of time on manual reviews/testing = $[cost]
- Bugs escaping to production = $[estimated cost]

**With Qodo:**
- [A]% time savings = $[savings]
- [B]% fewer production bugs = $[savings]
- Qodo cost: $[price]
- **Net savings: $[total]**
- **ROI: [X]%**

The question isn't whether you can afford Qodo - it's whether you can afford NOT to have it.

Can we trial with one team to prove the ROI?
```

### "Already Using [Competitor]"

```
That's great you're using [Competitor]! Many of our customers started there.

**Quick question:** How satisfied are you with:
- [Specific capability 1]?
- [Specific capability 2]?
- [Common competitor gap]?

The reason I ask: [X]% of teams using [Competitor] also add Qodo because we specialize in [differentiator].

**What Qodo adds:**
- [Differentiator 1 with metric]
- [Differentiator 2 with metric]
- [Differentiator 3 with metric]

Can we run a quick side-by-side comparison on your code? 30 minutes would show you the difference.
```

### "Developers Will Resist AI"

```
I hear this concern often! Here's what we see in practice:

**Week 1:** Skepticism ("I don't trust AI")
**Week 2:** Curiosity ("Let me try on this one PR")
**Week 3:** Adoption ("This actually catches things I miss")
**Month 2:** Advocacy ("Can we roll this out to more teams?")

**Why developers end up loving Qodo:**
- Saves them from boring, repetitive work
- Catches embarrassing bugs before others see them
- Makes them look good (higher quality code)
- Teaches them patterns they didn't know

**Mitigation approach:**
1. Start with volunteers, not mandates
2. Let data speak (show time saved, bugs caught)
3. Position as "teammate" not "replacement"
4. Share success stories from peer companies

Want to pilot with your most skeptical developer? They often become biggest advocates!
```

## Templates Usage Guide

**When to use each template:**

1. **Demo Follow-Up**: Within 2 hours of every demo
2. **Feature Announcement**: When new features match customer needs
3. **Check-In**: Every 4-6 weeks for active customers
4. **Trial Kick-Off**: At start of every trial/POC
5. **Technical Issue**: Immediately upon issue report
6. **Success Story**: When customer achieves significant results
7. **Renewal**: 60 days before renewal date
8. **Objection Handling**: Real-time during conversations
9. **Executive Summary**: Quarterly or upon request

**Customization Tips:**
- Always use customer's terminology
- Reference specific conversations
- Include relevant metrics
- Provide clear next steps
- Keep tone professional but personable
- Proofread twice before sending
